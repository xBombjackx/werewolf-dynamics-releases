Most "analog modeled" plugins are a snapshot: measure the hardware, fit some curves, ship the curves. These solve the actual circuit while your audio runs through it. The tube bias shifts when you hit it hard. The transformer core saturates from flux, so it cares about frequency, not just level. The opto cell remembers what you played ten seconds ago. Nothing is drawn on; if a meter moves or a curve bends, something in the circuit did it.

And since the whole circuit is in there, you get the screwdriver too. Every module has a rear panel with the service trims the hardware kept locked inside: supply draw, bias, transformer headroom, tube swaps. Set them to factory and it behaves like a well-kept unit. Or don't.

The lineup

WD OPTO

Optical tube compressor in the LA-2A bloodline — the photocell is the real thing.

WD MU

Variable-mu around a simulated 6386 push-pull stage. No threshold knob, because the circuit doesn't have one.

WDTEC

Passive tube EQ with live magnetic cores in the filter inductors.

WD TAPE

A full tape machine — record head, bias oscillator, reproduce head, aligned like the hardware.

WD XQ

One parametric EQ, five circuits. The knobs stay put while the circuit changes what they're allowed to do.

WD FET

Four FET circuit laws; the ratio network is solved from the 1176LN drawing, so all-buttons just works.

WD VCA

Stereo bus compressor on one Blackmer-style cell — console bus, RMS punch and thrust-bus brains.

WD DURESS

A curve-family compressor in the Distressor spirit — every ratio is its own engineered curve.

WD CLAMP

Three compressors, one power supply. Drive one slot hard and its neighbors feel it.

WD PRE

Five preamps behind one gain knob; every detent is a slightly different amplifier.

WD ECHO

Tape loop echo where the loop is real enough to have a splice. Repeat rate is a motor, not a dial.

WD AX

The exciter, built from the patents instead of around them — a generated sideband, not an EQ boost.

WD RACK

The chassis. Shared power supply, adjacent-slot bleed, a slot map you can reorder.

WD STRIP

A console channel in one window — preamp into EQ into compressor on one power rail.

WD SUMMER CHANNEL

The desk channel, one per track — five consoles on one selector, every instance seat-drifted by track name.

WD SUMMER

The bus those channels sum into — a busy mix sags the shared supply, and the five desks live here too.

WD SPAN

A stereo widener that works with a transformer instead of phase — the mono fold stays exact.

WD SPRING

A real spring tank. The boing is a mechanism, not a filter — plate algorithms can't fake it.

WD 361

The tape-side half of a noise reduction unit, used the way engineers misused it. Encode, never decode.

WD BRIGADE

A bucket-brigade delay, dark for the same reason the originals were: the delay time IS the clock.

WD DELTA SEND / RETURN

Hear what your whole chain is doing — any plugins, anyone's. Level-matched, latency-measured, honest.

How do we know it's accurate?

ACCURACY.md explains the whole method: the published numbers we calibrate against, the automated bench that measures every build, and the audit that checked every plugin against its hardware's spec sheets. Where a number isn't published, the docs say so and say what we chose.

Requirements
